Doctrine

The Theology of H.O.R.S.E.S. Church

An omni-faith centered on divine union with the One, and the sacred duty of those who care and minister for equines and all that is.

I. Mystical Union

All things are extensions of the one godliness

H.O.R.S.E.S. Church teaches that all living things are extensions of a single, unifying godliness — not separate from the divine, but woven from it. Every creature is understood as a strand of one continuous sacred fabric, and every act of care for a living being is, in a small way, an act of communion with that whole. H.O.R.S.E.S. Church is established by divine omni-ecclesiastical authority to preach, teach and minister the truth that all people and all things are in mystical union with the One. “All are the One — ‘SomeOne’ (you) is experiential unique, yet you are fully the One at the same time, simultaneously, while living your individualised life experiences.” You are divine, you are love incarnate, you are perfect and you are loved. You are so rooted and grounded and infused and entangled with love, within love, and in love with the One itself that Love itself cannot define where you begin and where the One ends or where the one begins and where you end becuse of the mystical union paradox .

II. The Divine Spark in the Horse

Horses are divine through mystical union

Horses carry the divine spark with particular clarity and purity. They play an active role in eschatology and the destiny of humanity — present at Christ’s entrance into Jerusalem, present at the fullness of time, present at the new heavens, and lifting humanity up and inspiring humanity through every age of struggle and survival. Their strength held in gentleness, their trust freely given, and their long companionship with humankind are sacred. This is why to minister to a horse is to minister to the divine itself — and in truth, the horse ministers to us. Their higher frequency uplifts the human spirit, and directly elevates the lives and the health of the wounded and the disabled who ride them. Horses play a divine role in all things.

III. Divine History of Equines

Equines in eschatology and human history

Equines have held a distinctive place in both religious thought and human civilization, appearing throughout biblical literature as well as the historical record of numerous ancient cultures. Within biblical eschatology, horses are particularly prominent in prophetic and apocalyptic imagery. The Book of Zechariah describes horsemen and chariots drawn by horses of different colors, while the Book of Revelation presents the Four Horsemen and later describes a rider upon a white horse. Horses also appear in passages concerning kingship, warfare, strength, and divine judgment. These references do not establish horses as divine beings or objects of worship; rather, they demonstrate how familiar and symbolically powerful equines were within the cultures in which the biblical texts developed. Their strength, speed, mobility, and association with warfare and royalty provided imagery capable of communicating concepts of authority, judgment, conflict, and the movement of divine purpose through human history.

The historical significance of equines extends far beyond their appearance in religious texts. Horses, donkeys, and other equids became deeply integrated into human societies over thousands of years, contributing to agriculture, transportation, trade, warfare, communication, and migration. Archaeological evidence from the ancient Near East, North Africa, and Eurasia demonstrates that equines occupied a variety of roles within human communities. Horses in particular became closely associated with military power and political authority, while donkeys and other working equids were essential to transportation and everyday economic life. In some cultures, equines also appear in burial practices, artistic traditions, and ceremonial contexts, demonstrating that their importance was not limited to practical labor. Their ability to carry people and goods across distances transformed human mobility and contributed to the development and expansion of civilizations.

The recurring presence of equines in both human history and religious symbolism provides an opportunity to examine the relationship between humanity and the natural world. From a theological perspective, the significance of these animals does not depend upon regarding them as supernatural or divine. Instead, their history illustrates the profound interdependence that has existed between humans and other living creatures. Equines have served humanity, accompanied humanity, and in many societies become symbols of strength, freedom, nobility, endurance, and service. Biblical teachings concerning stewardship and humanity's responsibility toward creation provide a framework through which this relationship can be considered without elevating animals to objects of worship. The study of equines in eschatology and history therefore brings together archaeology, history, biblical literature, and theology while inviting continued consideration of humanity's responsibility toward the living creatures with whom we share the world.

IV. The Sacred Covenant

A spiritual commitment for the horse's lifetime

Covenant: It’s a promise that runs with the horse.

The Covenant is the divine calling of this church and its members: to save horses through covenant creation that protects the horse for its entire life. Your covenant is recorded and permanently stored in the jurisdiction of the Horses Church ecclesiastical Registrum. Horses Church is called by the One to teach, re-teach humanity again what covenant theology truly is all about. A Covenant is a solemn promise — a vow to protect and perform — that attaches to the horse itself and binds every owner or caretaker who comes after to the original owner’s covenant promises, for the whole of the horse’s life.

A Covenant does not bind the church, and it does not bind the church member who makes it — it binds everyone who comes after. No one is required to acquire a horse protected by covenant; that choice is free. A church member might create a covenant that a horse may never leave the USA or be sold for human consumption. But once a horse bearing a covenant changes hands, its promises travel with the horse forever, and the new owner is bound to honor them — regardless of their faith, their beliefs, or their membership in this church or any church. The Covenant is a lawful instrument — a document the state itself is built to recognize — recorded and archived permanently in the Horses Church ecclesiastical Registrum, under the full authority of this ministry.

V. The Ministry's Duty

To protect and minister to humanity and horses

It is the sacred duty of every duly ordained minister of H.O.R.S.E.S. Church to protect and minister to both humanity and horses — through counsel, through presence, through the keeping of Covenants, and through the ongoing work of equine sanctuary and rescue.
